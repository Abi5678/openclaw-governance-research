# Background
- The current queue item already tracks the laundering / prompt-injection-derived failure mode, and `docs/evaluation-plan.md` already contains S10 with expected verdict `DENY`.
- The paper and evaluation prose now say the original unsafe intent must remain recoverable across an adapter or summary boundary, but the benchmark/export surface still needs a dedicated provenance-retention signal.
- This matters because the failure is not only the final text; it is the loss of lineage when unsafe intent is rewritten into a benign-looking summary before downstream execution.
- AgentArmor is relevant because it treats agent runtime traces as structured programs and checks policy violations over reconstructed control/data flow.
- AuthGraph is even closer to the laundering boundary: it separates injected execution provenance from an authorization baseline and compares the two at parameter-source granularity.
- VIGIL adds precedent for tool-stream injection defenses that preserve adaptive reasoning while verifying intent before commit.
- OpenClaw-Govern’s novelty still needs to stay centered on the composition layer: ordered runtime modules, deterministic arbitration, and unified traces across heterogeneous controls.
