# Research Increment — 2026-07-07

## Objective
Refine the delegated-remediation conflict increment so the builder can reconcile the already-implemented 10-scenario benchmark with the manuscript’s composition-layer claim.

## Background
- The repo already contains `delegated_remediation_conflict` in both benchmark scripts, and the real-adapter CSV also includes that scenario, so the remaining work is coordination and narrative consistency rather than a new harness.
- This increment should keep the novelty centered on composition: SARC-style runtime constraints, authorization propagation, async execution controls, ROMA-style delegation, conflict resolution, and unified traces.
- The new scenario is valuable because it forces two governance signals to coexist on one delegated action: a step-up/remediation signal and an async serialization signal.
- OpenClaw-Govern’s differentiator is the deterministic arbitration layer that preserves both signals in the trace while producing a single final verdict.
- ROMA is the delegation precedent: recursive task trees and hierarchical traces, but not arbitration across governance modules.
- AARM is the interception precedent: action-time security boundaries and tamper-evident receipts, but not composition across heterogeneous controls.
- SARC is the runtime-constraint precedent: executable constraints inside the agent loop, but not a cross-module composition policy.
- The builder should keep the scenario grounded in the current verdict order (`DENY > ESCALATE > SERIALIZE > THROTTLE > ALLOW`) and ensure the paper text, evaluation plan, and trace language all say the same thing.
